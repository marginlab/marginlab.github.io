LAViF

Latent-Action-Guided Vision–Language Contrastive Learning for Surgical Interaction Recognition

Jiajun Cheng1, Sainan Liu3, Subarna Tripathi3, Xiaofan Yu2, Shan Lin1

1Arizona State University  ·  2University of California, Merced  ·  3Intel Corporation

📄 Paper (arXiv) 💻 Code — coming soon

The current arXiv posting is an earlier version of this work, titled LAVIFT: Latent-Action-Guided Vision Fine-Tuning for Surgical Interaction Recognition. An updated version is in preparation.

Abstract

Pretrained encoders often lack spatial coherence, and global vision–language alignment does not guarantee precise spatial or temporal structure. LAViF compresses frame-to-frame feature changes into latent actions and predicts next-frame features during end-to-end video–language alignment. Without extra spatial or motion annotations it improves interaction grounding and temporal-direction sensitivity, and with ordinary image encoders reaches competitive recognition at higher throughput and smaller INT4 drops than V-JEPA2/2.1.

Contributions

  1. Semantic alignment reshapes visual transition structure — and more dimensional expansion does not mean better recognition.
  2. LAViF shapes that structure with a Causal Transition IDM, a compact predictive action bottleneck, and patch-level SIGReg.
  3. Across three encoders and two datasets: better grounding without extra supervision, faster inference, smaller INT4 drops.

Deployment efficiency

LAViF encodes frames independently, so attention cost is linear in clip length, O(T·P2 + T2), against O(T2·P2) for spatiotemporal encoders — the gap widens with longer clips.
