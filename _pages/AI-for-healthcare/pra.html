PRA

Predictive Rubric Alignment for Video-Based Surgical Skill Assessment

Jiajun Cheng1, Haidar M. Abdul-Muhsin2, Juha Choi1, Shan Lin1

1Arizona State University  ·  2Mayo Clinic

📄 Preprint — coming soon 💻 Code — coming soon

Abstract

Surgical skill assessment is essential for training, yet automated methods commonly use classifiers or regressors tied to a fixed rubric. Vision–language models offer an opportunity to assess skill through natural-language descriptions of performance levels. We explore an alternative to probability-based scoring with Predictive Rubric Alignment (PRA), which learns to predict a rater-weighted combination of level-description embeddings. Connecting adjacent descriptions defines a score-parameterized path, and the predicted embedding is mapped to its closest point on this path to read the score. PRA supports fixed rubric items through learned queries, while PRA-cond derives queries from item names, allowing new rubrics to be supplied as text without item-specific parameters. On AIxSuture, PRA outperforms published baselines and same-encoder description-matching and regression controls in skill classification. Trained on only two AIxSuture items, PRA-cond transfers zero-shot to JIGSAWS items not used in training, yielding positive score correlations and greater Grad-CAM overlap with instruments and suture than a description-matching baseline. These results support predictive rubric alignment as a promising direction for flexible and explainable surgical skill assessment.

Contributions

  1. PRA, an alternative formulation for VLM-based surgical skill assessment that supports fixed and text-specified rubric items, with potential to adapt to rubric items not used in training.
  2. Strong performance on AIxSuture, and improved visual grounding, measured by Grad-CAM overlap with instruments and suture, during zero-shot transfer to JIGSAWS.
